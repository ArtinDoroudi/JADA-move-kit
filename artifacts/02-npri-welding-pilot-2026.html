Substance thresholds

The five Parts, and how big each trigger is

Each Part has its own trigger and its own basis. Some count the material a shop handles. Others count only what is released to air. The scale below is logarithmic: each gridline is ten times the last.

Material handled (manufactured, processed or otherwise used) Released to air Released, disposed or sent for recycling
1APart 1A: core substances (Group A)10 tonnes handled, at 1% concentration or more+

Who

Fixed sites and offshore installations with 20,000+ employee hours, or with an activity that removes the hours gate.

Trigger

10 tonnes manufactured, processed or otherwise used, counted only where concentration is at least 1%. By-products count at any concentration. Equal to the threshold counts.

Then report

Releases, disposals and transfers for recycling, in tonnes, even if they are zero.

In a welding shop

Metals in electrodes, wire and steel stock, such as manganese, nickel and chromium compounds. Stock that passes through cutting, grinding and welding is counted once, not once per operation.

1BPart 1B: lower thresholds for specific substancesFor example 50 kg for cobalt, lead and hexavalent chromium; 5 kg for mercury+

Who

Same facility gates as Part 1A. Exception: facilities under the chromium electroplating, anodizing and reverse-etching regulations report hexavalent chromium regardless of hours, quantity or concentration.

Trigger

Each row has its own values. Cobalt, lead and hexavalent chromium are 50 kg at 0.1%. Mercury is 5 kg with no concentration test. Some groups are totalled, such as 20 benzothiazoles (100 kg) and cyanides (1,000 kg).

Then report

Releases, disposals and transfers for recycling in kilograms.

In a welding shop

Stainless electrodes produce hexavalent chromium fume. Lead in stainless steel, brass and bronze is excluded from the lead threshold. Older ECCC material calls cobalt Part 1A, but the current list puts it in 1B.

1CPart 1C: PFAS, new since 20251 kg at 0.1%, for each of 163 listed PFAS separately+

Who

Same Part 1 facility gates.

Trigger

Each listed PFAS on its own: 1 kg at 0.1% or more. Two PFAS below 1 kg each are not added together.

Then report

Releases, disposals and recycling transfers in kg, even if zero.

In a welding shop

Check mist suppressants in plating, firefighting foams and some specialty coatings or lubricants. An SDS that doesn't mention PFAS is not proof of absence. Unresolved formulations stay pending.

2Part 2: PAHs50 kg total across all listed PAHs, incidentally made and released+

Who

Fixed, portable and offshore facilities with 20,000+ hours or an hours-exempt activity. Creosote wood preservation is a separate branch with no threshold.

Trigger

Total mass of all listed PAHs released, disposed or recycled because of incidental manufacture reaches 50 kg. This is a total, not 50 kg per PAH.

Then report

Individual PAHs at 5 kg or more are reported separately; the rest as unspeciated total. Naphthalene is Part 1A, not Part 2.

In a welding shop

Rare. The welding calculator has no Part 2 data, but that doesn't prove Part 2 is absent. Combustion and burning-off of coatings are the usual places to look.

3Part 3: dioxins, furans and hexachlorobenzeneTriggered by specific activities only, regardless of quantity+

Who

Facilities doing a listed activity, such as waste incineration, base-metal or secondary aluminum smelting, steelmaking electric arc furnaces, cement, or pentachlorophenol wood preservation.

Trigger

The activity itself. Some activities apply regardless of hours; others also need 20,000 hours.

Then report

Individual congeners if known, otherwise a toxic-equivalent total. If it truly cannot be determined, "no information available", never zero.

In a welding shop

An arc welder is not a steelmaking electric arc furnace. We screen for real specialist operations (P26) instead of matching on the word "arc".

4Part 4: criteria air contaminantsAir releases: PM2.5 0.3 t, PM10 0.5 t, total VOC 10 t, CO, NOx, SO2 and TPM 20 t+

Who

All facility types. No 20,000-hour precondition. Below 20,000 hours (with no exempt activity), only stationary fuel-combustion releases count toward the threshold.

Trigger

ContaminantTo air
PM2.50.3 t
PM100.5 t
Total VOC10 t
CO, NOx, SO2, TPM20 t each

Then report

Air releases by category and method, monthly shares, whether particulate includes condensable PM, operating schedule, and shutdowns longer than a week.

In a welding shop

Welding fume, blasting dust, heaters and paint solvents all feed Part 4. TPM, PM10 and PM2.5 are nested sizes of the same dust, not three amounts to add. Unpaved-road dust counts above 10,000 vehicle-km a year. Small external-combustion equipment (under 10 million BTU/h, natural gas, propane or No. 1–2 oil only) can be exempt, but only when that is the substance's only source.

5Part 5: individual VOCs1 tonne released to air, for each listed VOC or group+

Who

All facility types, with the same low-hours rule as Part 4 (combustion only below 20,000 hours).

Trigger

Each listed VOC or group: 1 tonne released to air. It is not conditional on meeting the total-VOC threshold.

Then report

Tonnes per substance, methods, monthly shares and reasons for change. The same quantity may also appear under Part 1.

In a welding shop

Solvents in paints, thinners and degreasers. A booth filter catches paint particles but does not reduce solvent vapour.

StkIndividual stacksExtra detail only for tall, hot, vertical stacks+

When

Vertical stacks at least 25 m above grade, with measured annual exit temperature above 50 °C, and stack releases above the Notice Table 4 minimums.

Not included

Horizontal outlets, rain-capped stacks and tank vents don't get individual stack fields, but their releases still count in the air totals.

Conditional questionnaire

Plain questions, four honest answers

Clients see ordinary wording such as "Do you sandblast?", never "Does Part 1B apply?". Every screen offers four answers, and there is no pre-selected "No". Try one:

Activity screen · P08
Did anyone do sand, grit, shot, bead or wet blasting at this location in 2026, even occasionally?

Quantities are stored with unit, period and basis (used, purchased or estimated). "Records unavailable" and "ask JADA for help" are always allowed. A missing quantity is never filled in as zero.

      ExWorked routing examplesWhat happens in four situations we expect to see+

      Blasting found late

      P08 flips to Yes after approval. The blasting questions open, the facility particulate and metals totals update, and the approved package becomes a new draft.

      No chimney

      We still ask about extractors, booths and heaters. If no outlet exists, fugitive releases from each activity are still assessed. No tall stack doesn't mean no emissions.

      Unknown powder chemistry

      Powder use is known but curing emissions aren't. A supplier evidence task opens. Other calculations continue, and affected conclusions stay pending.

      Offsite painting

      If parts are painted by another company at its own site, JADA documents the boundary decision and onsite paint calculations may not apply. Contractor painting on the client's own property follows a different path and stays in.

      Ground rules

      Twelve shortcuts we will not take

      These come straight from the source review. Tap a card to see the rule.

      Your expertise needed

      Open questions for environmental and HSE review

      The sources disagree or are silent on these points. Until a practitioner decides, the software routes them to manual review instead of guessing.

      I03
      Individual-stack NOx threshold: 10 t or 20 t?

      The Notice (Schedule 4, Table 4) says 10 tonnes. The Guide (Appendix D, Table 18) says 20 tonnes. The Notice prevails, but we want a recorded interpretation before automating it.

      Do we apply 10 t, and should we seek ECCC clarification?

      I04
      Stack temperature: unmeasured, and exactly 50 °C

      The Notice says "measured to be above 50 °C". The Guide says unmeasured stacks qualify and uses "50 °C or more". These treat both unknown and equal values differently.

      How should we treat a stack with only a permit or estimated temperature?

      Cr
      Chromium basis in the ECCC welding calculator

      The calculator has separate chromium and hexavalent chromium rows. It doesn't say whether its "chromium" factor means total chromium or chromium excluding Cr(VI). The NPRI list treats them as different substances.

      Which basis do the welding factors use, and how do we split them?

      Pb
      Lead in stainless steel after it becomes fume

      Lead contained in stainless steel, brass and bronze is excluded from the lead threshold. It's unclear whether that exclusion still applies once the alloy is welded into fume.

      Does the alloy exclusion survive the welding transformation?

      1B
      Group concentration when one product has several members

      For groups such as benzothiazoles or cyanides, an SDS may give one concentration for a mixture of members. The source text doesn't settle whether the 0.1% test applies per member or to the mixture.

      Per member or combined?

      I05
      Fuel-oil heater calculator units

      The ECCC boiler and heater calculator shows a factor per thousand US gallons. The unit scale needs a dimensional check before we port it.

      Can someone verify the calculator's conversion against AP-42?

      I07
      Solvent evaporation shortcut

      Our internal VOC note uses "use × VOC content × evaporation fraction". There is no sourced, universal evaporation fraction. We plan a balance instead: input minus stock change, waste, recovery and product retention.

      Is a mass balance acceptable as our default for degreasing and paint solvent?

      I11
      Blasting beyond open dry sand

      ECCC defaults are uncontrolled dry sand on mild steel, varying with wind speed. Cabinets, recycled shot and wet blasting are not represented.

      What evidence would you accept for an enclosed or recycled-media blaster?

      Pilot scenarios every package type must survive

      1. Arc welding with known consumables, then an unknown electrode to test the fallback
      2. Welding plus cutting sharing an alloy and a dust collector, with no double counting
      3. One stock batch cut, ground and welded, keeping its lineage with no duplicate purchase
      4. Welding, blasting, coating, cleaning and gas heating, totalled across processes
      5. A low-hours shop with fuel-burning equipment, testing the branches instead of a headcount shortcut
      6. Painting outsourced offsite, but contractor blasting onsite
      7. A positive specialist screen, an unknown composition and a missing method, so the package can't close early
      8. Captured dust, waste solvent, wastewater and an air outlet, testing destinations
      9. A stack/source conflict and a changed control configuration, testing review and revision
      10. A prior reporter that no longer meets any criteria: Does Not Meet Criteria workflow
      11. A Part 1 threshold met with zero releases, which still requires a report

      What the plan is built on
