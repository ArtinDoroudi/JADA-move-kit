NPRI All-Industry Scope
Rough scope estimate · NPRI, all reporting industries · Nov 2026

Covering every NPRI industry

Covering all of NPRI is roughly 10× the welding pilot in process count. If the platform is built once and calculation methods live in data, it isn't 10× the code. Most industries reuse the same core, the same handful of calculators, and a shared library of everyday processes like boilers, tanks, painting and dust. The real cost is in about 240 sector-specific processes, each of which needs a practitioner to check it.

~16sector packs cover 7,419 of the 7,738 facilities that reported for 2024
~280distinct process families. That's 488 process slots before sharing, so sharing saves ~42%.
115–161person-months total. That's 10–13 person-years, or about 2 years with a team of 7.
~260k vs ~1.9Mlines of app code: data-driven design vs. continuing the current per-case style

3 · Priority

Which industry to cover next

Every industry is scored on two things: how many likely clients it has (real 2024 NPRI facility counts, weighted toward small owners who don't have an in-house environmental team), and how much it costs to build. The best picks sit top-left: lots of clients for little effort.

3,503 · 171Oil and gas is 45% of all facilities, but only 171 companies. 97% of those facilities belong to multi-site operators, about 20 each. That's an enterprise or consultant sale, not one shop at a time.
580 · 430Welding and metal fabrication is the right first market. 430 separate companies, 86% of them small owners, about 5 substances per facility. The pilot choice holds up.
25–33Substances per facility in refining, oil sands and pulp mills. Few facilities, heavy speciation, and buyers who already use consultants. Build these only when a client pays for the pack.

Return index = facilities × (0.4 + 0.6 × share of facilities owned by companies with three or fewer sites). Small owners rarely have in-house environmental staff, so they're the likeliest buyers. Facilities of large multi-site operators still count, at 40%. Score = return index ÷ midpoint effort, scaled so the best industry is 100. Upstream oil and gas scores second but goes in Wave 2: it needs four new calculators and an enterprise sales motion first. The data covers only facilities that already report. Facilities that should report but don't aren't counted.

4 · Effort

Where the person-months go

Each bar covers the low-to-high range. The darker part is the low estimate and the lighter tail runs to the high estimate. Person-months include practitioner verification, which AI can't speed up.

Ongoing cost after the build: about 3–5 person-months a year to track the next Gazette notice (the current one runs 2025–2027), substance-list changes and factor updates. AI help could cut the developer share by roughly 30–40%. It doesn't shorten the practitioner review, which is about a third of each process estimate.

5 · Why the architecture decides the size

Same coverage, about 7× less code

Continue the current style

~1.9M lines
  • v0 is ~65k lines of app code for ~60% of 28 families, or ~3.9k lines per finished family
  • × 488 process slots, because without shared modules nothing is reused
  • No team can review this, and every new notice means touching all of it

Data-driven core

~260k lines
  • Core ~45k + method engine ~20k
  • 45 shared modules × ~1.2k (questions, config, tests)
  • 236 sector processes × ~0.6k, mostly data rows and golden cases
  • New industries add data, not engine code

Both figures are order-of-magnitude estimates. The point is the ratio, not the exact count.

Rules for every cycle

  1. One industry pack per cycle. Don't start the next pack until the current gate passes.
  2. A gate means practitioner-signed golden cases, not lines of code or test counts.
  3. No new features during filing season (mid-March to June 1).
  4. Give Codex or any other agent one cycle-sized task at a time and commit each slice. A person then reviews 1–3k lines per cycle instead of 100k at the end.
  5. Re-score the priority table each summer with the new NPRI data and pilot feedback.
Assumptions, method and what to verify
  • Client counts: from ECCC's 2024 single-year facility file (7,738 facilities, 2,301 companies), grouped by NAICS code into the 16 packs. 319 facilities (airports, defence, services, wholesale) fall outside the packs. "Small owner" means a company with three or fewer reporting facilities. Company names were counted as written, so a few spelling variants of the same company may be counted separately.
  • Sectors and processes: the 16 packs and their process counts are my estimates. They draw on NAICS sector structure, ECCC's sector tools (sour gas SO₂, storage tanks, pits and quarries, tailings and waste rock, wood products, potash) and its general fugitive guidance (abrasive blasting, aggregate handling, wet cooling towers).
  • Effort per process: about 1.5 person-weeks per sector-specific process in a data-driven design (~1 week developer + ~0.5 week practitioner). Shared modules cost more (~1.5–2 weeks) because they must work across sectors.
  • Calibration: the welding pilot has 28 families. v0 reached ~60% of its contract with ~65k app lines. The remaining 8–13 weeks from the earlier audit is the anchor for "welding (finish)".
  • Not included: GHG reporting (a separate federal program), provincial programs, sales and support, hosting, and a regulatory-change buffer.
  • Special NPRI rules per sector (pits and quarries production threshold, no hours threshold for wood preservation, terminal operations, incineration and large wastewater systems) are in the regulatory core. They're cheap to code but need practitioner sign-off.
