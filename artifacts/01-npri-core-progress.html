NPRI Core progress

We are building the official ECCC welding calculator into the app. Client documents fill in its inputs automatically, the app decides every NPRI threshold, and a JADA admin reviews one summary page with the reasoning behind each decision. The calculation engine is built and runs end to end. The target is a fully working release for welding shops by 31 March 2027, two months before the 1 June filing deadline.

Milestones

  1. 1

    Safe workspace

    Done12 Nov

    A separate copy of the code and a separate test database. The live platform has not been touched; this is checked after every step.

  2. 2

    The official workbook inside the app

    Done13 Nov

    All 733 ECCC welding, cutting and spraying factors are imported cell by cell, each one traceable to its workbook cell, along with the same formulas, including spraying and Method 2.

  3. 3

    Decision engine, end to end

    Done17 Nov

    Eight steps turn the inputs into a full NPRI answer, covering totals, the robustness check on thresholds, reporting duties, the PFAS and dioxin rules, 35 filing-field checks and a readiness list for the admin. Runs are saved and can be replayed exactly for audit. A test welding shop ran through all eight steps, and its numbers matched a hand calculation (PM10 95.64 kg).

    Also done: the first client screen, which shows the 18 core questions on desktop and mobile.

    Demo ready (17 Nov): the admin case summary, with every decision, its reason and a click-through to the calculation, and the Excel-style workbook. Change the wire used from 1,200 to 2,000 kg and PM10 updates at once from 95.64 to 98.2 kg. The change is logged and needs a second staff check before approval.

  4. 4

    Proven equal to the official Excel

    Done17 Nov

    393 of 393 scenarios match the government workbook: all 74 welding, cutting and spraying categories at five quantity sizes, plus the guide's worked examples and edge cases. That is 536,446 cell comparisons in 7 seconds, with the largest difference at the level of computer rounding (4 × 10-16). The check also caught and fixed one nickel routing error the engine had.

  5. 5

    Smart filling

    Startingby 18 Dec

    Defaults are approved once by HSE. Inputs clients don't know are derived from purchases, for example grinding hours from discs bought, cutting hours from plasma tips, or electrode kg from boxes. The app only asks a question when the answer could change a threshold decision.

    The test shop's 71 open items break down as 31 answered by the client questionnaire, 15 filing details, 6 from documents, 13 software gaps still to build, 3 defaults to approve and 3 practitioner decisions. Most are things the client fills in, not exceptions for HSE. The target is 5 or fewer HSE exceptions per report.

  2. HSE practitioner approves the defaults list, which smart filling depends on. by 11 Dec
  3.   4. HSE practitioner signs 12 test cases and keeps two weeks free for the rehearsal. 26 Feb · 1–12 Mar
  5. Enough AI coding capacity. The coding agent's weekly limits are the main pacing factor for the build.

Limits and risks

  • The schedule keeps 15–31 March for rehearsal fixes. If the documents or decisions arrive more than two weeks late, the release moves into April.
  • A 31 March release leaves pilot clients April and May to file. Clients who want to file early can start once the rehearsal passes in mid-March.
  • The app produces the filing package. Entering it in ECCC's online reporting system stays a human step.
  • PAHs (Part 2) and individual VOCs (Part 5) are flagged for manual handling at release. Typical welding shops rarely reach them.
  • When an input is unknown, the app marks the result as not robust and never assumes zero. Early runs will look strict until smart filling lands.
  • Each build step has come in 25–40% larger than estimated. Files are still kept small and reviewable.
