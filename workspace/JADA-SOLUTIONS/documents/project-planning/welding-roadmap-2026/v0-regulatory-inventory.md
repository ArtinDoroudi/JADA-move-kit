## Facility-level decision gates before substance rules

Decide and retain facts by facility and calendar year; a process or substance calculation is not a facility filing decision.

1. **Facility category and boundary.** N1 Schedule 2 defines contiguous, portable, pipeline and offshore installation. Under N1 Schedule 3 §6, Part 1 applies to contiguous facilities and offshore installations; §8 Part 2 and §10 Part 3 apply to contiguous, portable and offshore facilities; Parts 4–5 apply to contiguous, portable, pipeline and offshore installations. Confirm the applicable type and do not run every substance part for every facility type. For a typical fixed fabrication shop, this is normally a contiguous facility, but the category must be evidenced.
2. **Employee-hour trigger and scope.** For Parts 1 and 2, the general gate is at least 20,000 employee hours, unless an enumerated employee-threshold-exempt activity occurs. Count all employee hours under G1 §3.4.3 (employees regardless of function/location, owner hours spent working onsite, contractor hours related to facility operations, paid overtime, vacation and sick leave are included; guide examples also include students and part-time/term workers). It is total hours, not a headcount or FTE shortcut.
3. **Employee-threshold-exempt activities.** N1 Schedule 3 §1(1)(a): non-hazardous solid-waste incineration ≥26 tonnes; biomedical/hospital-waste incineration ≥26 tonnes; hazardous-waste incineration; sewage-sludge incineration; wood preservation; terminal operations; wastewater collection-system discharge to surface water averaging ≥10,000 m³/day; or pit/quarry production ≥500,000 tonnes. When one occurs, the employee-hours gate does not apply to Parts 1 and 2 (and it is one of the Part 3 activity branches). The substance-specific criteria still apply. Report and retain explicit present/absent/unknown determinations.
4. **Other general triggers and exclusions.** N1 Schedule 3 §1 also brings in facilities subject to the Chromium Electroplating, Chromium Anodizing and Reverse Etching Regulations, low-hours facilities meeting Part 4 criteria, and pipeline installations. Section 1(2) excludes a facility whose *only* activities are specified oil/gas exploration or well drilling, low-volume wastewater discharge, or quarry production below 500,000 tonnes. §§2–3 prescribe activity/material exclusions in threshold calculations; these are scoped rules, not a generic “shop use” exemption. Section 2(2) explicitly prevents treating vehicle painting/stripping and rebuilding/remanufacturing vehicle components as excluded vehicle maintenance.
5. **Use legal definitions and record lineage.** Assess each calendar year independently, use Schedule 2 definitions for manufacture/process/otherwise use, by-product, article, disposal and related terms, and avoid counting the same material twice when it flows through sequential operations. N1 Schedule 3 §4 says qualifying disposal counts in Part 1 and 2 mass threshold calculations but is not a release; §5 assigns the full year to the Dec 31 owner/operator, and the last owner/operator reports the portion operated when operations terminate.

## Shared rules that affect multiple Parts

### Exclusions and threshold contributions

N1 Schedule 3 §§2–4 and G1 §3.5 specify scope. Among the items to model separately are student education/training, research/development, vehicle maintenance/repair, fuel distribution/storage/retail except terminal operations, sale of articles/products containing a substance, retail sale of substances, renewable natural resource activity, dentistry, specified low-volume sectors, and specified contained materials (articles; structural facility materials but not process equipment; janitorial/grounds materials; personal-use materials; intake water/air; overburden; qualifying clean/inert waste rock; unchanged inert/inorganic tailings components). Vehicle painting/stripping and rebuild/remanufacture of vehicle components are not covered by the vehicle-maintenance exclusion. Exclusions from threshold input do not generically remove later release outputs: retain the excluded amount and scope decision with its source, then follow the Part’s report-output clause.

For Part 1, MPO is substance quantity manufactured, processed or otherwise used. Quantities should not be counted twice across process steps; a release that arose from a counted process quantity is not another independent MPO amount. By-product scope is specific: qualifying incidentally manufactured/processed/otherwise-used substance released or disposed counts toward Part 1 mass threshold regardless of concentration; recycled by-product or material remaining in final product is not a by-product for this purpose (G1 §5.1.2). Tailings and waste-rock treatment has distinct statutory clauses. Avoid one universal “SDS percentage × purchases” rule.

### Identity, concentration, and groups

- Keep part/group/member and source qualifiers as structured data keyed to stable NPRI identity and reporting-year version. Preserve non-CAS NPRI identifiers and group membership; do not treat row order or chemical similarity as identity.
- Concentration is a gate on the amount used in the MPO threshold, not a second independent amount to add. For Group A ordinary MPO, the listed 1% rule generally applies; Part 1B and 1C use each table row’s rule. For a mixture concentration range, G1 §5.2.2 says ask the supplier for more detail and, if unavailable, use the range average for threshold determination. Preserve the original range/evidence and the adopted average so this is reviewable.
- Preserve qualifier-specific accounting. Examples in G1 §5.2.1: “all isomers” aggregate; metal “and its compounds” can require element-equivalent mass; lead excludes the lead contribution from tetraethyl lead, stainless steel, brass and bronze from the lead threshold and treats tetraethyl lead separately; salts may be reported as equivalent acid/base mass; total reduced sulphur is H2S-equivalent and only air output is reportable; aluminum oxide is fibrous forms only; asbestos is friable only; aluminum is fume/dust only; nitrate ion is solution pH ≥6; ionic cyanide includes salts but excludes organocyanides/nitriles/organometallic cyanides; “total” ammonia/phosphorus have additional definitions. This is a selector into specific semantics, not an exhaustive substitute for Schedule 1 notes.
- Group threshold aggregation must follow the Notice/list row: some groups are one threshold subject, while Part 1C PFAS are individually thresholded. Part 5 is a separate reportable identity/group system. Do not automatically sum members across a group or report all members individually unless the current qualifier says so.
- Guide rule examples for Part 1A say count quantities that were at/above concentration at any time or place at the facility, including after dilution or before concentration; an average is the stated default for an unavailable concentration range. The Notice controls if a future discrepancy is found.

### Stacks and air-output fields

Basic outlet/point-source inventory is required to support every air method. Individual stack details are conditional and should be evaluated substance-by-substance only after the facility reporting criteria are met.

- Notice Schedule 4 §§20 and 23 trigger individual Part 4/5 stack fields for vertical stacks ≥25 m above grade when the relevant stack substance quantity meets its Notice Table 4 minimum **and** measured annual average exit temperature is above 50 °C. The Notice’s listed Part 4 stack minimums are CO 10 t, NOx 10 t, PM2.5 0.25 t, PM10 0.5 t, SO2 25 t, TPM 25 t, total VOC 5 t. Part 5 uses ≥0.25 t for each speciated VOC. The Notice excludes horizontal/non-vertical stacks, stacks with rain caps, and storage-tank stacks/vents from these individually reported stack fields; their releases remain in reportable air totals. Schedule 4 requires stack physical/flow/temperature identifiers and disaggregated fuel/source categories.
- **Unresolved Notice/guide conflict:** G1 Appendix D Table 18 lists a 20 t NOx individual-stack threshold, while N1 Schedule 4 Table 4 says 10 t. N1 prevails. This inventory records the Notice value as controlling source text, but automated stack assessment should remain flagged for JADA/regulator interpretation before release due to an established guide inconsistency.
- **Unresolved temperature wording:** N1 says the temperature criterion applies where annual average exit temperature “is measured to be above 50 °C” and also requires reporting temperature if known, permit/approval value, or unknown. G1 Appendix D says unmeasured temperature qualifies and says only measured values can be compared, with ≥50 °C; these are conflicting treatment of unknown and equality. Do not implement a guessed comparator or fallback. Retain measured-vs-estimated-vs-permit-vs-unknown evidence and route borderline/unknown stack cases to a reviewed interpretation.
- Individual stack “thresholds” are distinct from facility-wide CAC/Part 5 reporting thresholds. For the facility-wide gate, use Schedule 3 thresholds and correct source case; Schedule 4 stack rows do not replace facility-wide reporting.

### Methods, reported quantities, and JADA approval

Schedule 4 §2 permits CEM, predictive monitoring, source testing, remote quantification, mass balance, published/site-specific emission factors, speciation profile or engineering estimates, subject to its required-measurement rule (§1). Calculations should keep (a) threshold-assessment quantity, (b) emissions/releases, (c) disposal and (d) transfer for recycling separate, with method and source trace. A published factor is usable only when its method, process, substance, basis, units and conditions match the case and a reviewer accepts that path. A published ECCC factor is not a JADA-approved method by virtue of being published.

## Annual filing and retention cases

The report status should be decided for every year, not carried forward as a static facility flag. The Gazette Notice opening provisions and Schedule 4 general §§1–6, the current guide §§2–3.2, and ECCC’s reporting workflow support the following cases:

| Annual facts/outcome | Workflow case for 2026 | Key implementation behavior |
|---|---|---|
| One or more Part criteria met | NPRI substance report for each qualifying substance/part; submit by **June 1, 2027**. Include required zero releases/disposals/transfers for a substance once its reporting criterion is met. Include a Sales/Closure/Purchase report too where applicable. | Keep draft, reviewed, certified, submitted, and filing-confirmation states distinct. Capture facility/non-calculation fields from Schedule 4 §§7–9. |
| 2025 NPRI substance report was filed, but no 2026 criterion is met and no sale/closure/purchase case supersedes | **Does Not Meet Criteria (DNMC)** report by June 1, 2027, with reason; if due to pollution prevention activity, identify that. | “Nothing reportable this year” is not a no-action conclusion for a prior-year reporter. Keep evidence of each now-false criterion. |
| Facility sold, closed, or purchased during year and criteria are not met | Sales/Closure/Purchase report if applicable; Notice §5 identifies the year-end owner and termination rule for substance reporting. | Determine legal owner/operator and operation dates; do not silently file ordinary DNMC only. |
| First-time/non-prior reporter, no criteria met, and no sale/closure/purchase reporting case | No mandatory NPRI substance report established by these criteria; retain a substantiated no-filing decision. | Separate from voluntary report option. Do not fabricate a “zero report.” |
| Criteria not met but voluntary reporting chosen | Voluntary NPRI report, flagged as voluntary in SWIM. | Keep voluntary status separate from mandatory applicability. |

The person who owns or operates the facility on December 31 reports for the full calendar year; for terminated operations, the last owner/operator reports for the period in operation. Keep copies of required information, calculations, measurements and supporting data at the facility or Canadian principal place of business for three years from the due date (opening notice §34). Reporting is per facility and year. The authorized Statement/electronic Certification certifies true, accurate and complete information; internal JADA review is a separate step.

## Open interpretation / release gates

1. **Individual stack NOx threshold:** Notice Table 4 10 t vs current Guide Appendix D Table 18 20 t. The Notice text controls; obtain/record a JADA-approved legal/regulatory interpretation or clarification before enabling automatic stack result.
2. **Stack annual-temperature comparator:** Notice “measured to be above 50 °C” differs from Guide Appendix D “not measured or ≥50 °C” and its note that only measured values may be compared. Equality and unknown/permit/default handling must be resolved before automatic stack assessment.
3. **Part 1 group concentration mechanics:** implement exact substances/group member tables and qualifiers from N1/W1/G1. Where concentration evidence is only attached to mixtures containing multiple group members, confirm the correct sum/eligibility semantics rather than guessing from aggregate data.
4. **Every exception needs a scoped record:** distinguish threshold-input exclusion, emission/output exception, activity-based no-hour gate, and filing workflow. A single generic `exempt` flag is not adequate.
5. **Factor coverage is separate from criteria coverage:** published factors are candidate methods. No JADA approval, current independent benchmark, or full process coverage is established by the workbook inspection recorded here.
6. **Version/currentness:** the source register checked Gazette notice index and current guide on 2026-11-04, but this inventory is not an exhaustive amendment audit. Recheck legal notices, amendments and reporting-system changes before rule release and before filing.
